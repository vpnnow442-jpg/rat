How to improve your reaction time

Your basic reaction speed is mostly set by your nervous system, so big changes are unlikely. You can still improve how reliably you perform and remove things that slow your score down.

Try the reaction test

These are practical tips, not the findings of one specific study. We have not tested them for you.

Check your setup first

  • A screen with a higher refresh rate and a wired mouse usually add less delay than a slow screen and a wireless mouse.
  • Close other tabs and apps, which can make a browser test less steady.
  • Test on the same device each time so you can compare your scores fairly.

Practise in short sessions

  • A few minutes a day is easier to keep up than one long session.
  • Do five or more tries and look at your average, not your single best.
  • Stop if you feel dizzy or get a headache.

Stay relaxed and focused

  • Many people react more steadily when they are rested and not distracted.
  • Keep your hand loose and ready. Tensing up tends to make timing worse.
  • Look at the middle of the screen so you can see changes anywhere.

Train the skill you want

Reaction time is not one skill. Reacting to a sound, picking the right arrow, and hitting a moving target all use different parts of the task. Mix the games to practise different kinds of reaction.
