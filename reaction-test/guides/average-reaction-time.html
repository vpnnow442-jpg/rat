Average reaction time: what the research says

There is no single "normal" reaction time. It depends on what you react to, how it is measured, and who is being tested. Here are the numbers we could find in published sources.

Test your own reaction time

Adults, simple visual reaction

A lab study of 1,469 adults aged 18 to 65 found an average simple visual reaction time of about 231 ms, or about 213 ms after correcting for equipment delay. Reaction time got slower by about 0.55 ms for each year of age. Woods et al. 2015.

Other lab samples from the UK, pooled in a published commentary, give averages of roughly 208 ms to 238 ms. The older figures come from very old equipment and are debated. Commentary on UK samples.

Sound vs sight

Reacting to a sound is usually a little faster than reacting to something you see. A review cited in one paper gives roughly 140 to 160 ms for sound and 180 to 200 ms for sight. A small study of 14 people found about 284 ms for sound and 331 ms for sight, so the exact numbers vary a lot. Review, small study.

Elite sprinters

At the 2008 Beijing Olympics, an analysis of 425 sprinters found average fastest-start reaction times of 166 ms for men and 189 ms for women. The fastest possible reactions in that study were about 109 ms and 121 ms. PLOS ONE sprinter study.

Online tests

Browser tests usually read higher than lab tests, because your screen, mouse and browser each add delay. One reaction time site reports a median of about 270 ms. Reaction time test benchmark article. This is a website's own figure, not a controlled study.

What to take from this

Compare yourself mainly with your own past scores on the same device. Differences of 10 to 20 ms between tries are normal.
